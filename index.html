A few of my latest creations

Music Bingo

I host music bingo at bars around Brooklyn, and got tired of copy-pasting a few hundred songs into web forms every week. So I built a set of command line tools that take a round from a themed song list to a PDF of randomized, printable cards in two commands: one script pulls a Spotify playlist into a text file, another drives BingoBaker with Puppeteer to generate the cards. I use Claude to draft the themed lists, which is the slow part of planning a night. It's open source under MIT. See it on GitHub. 

node scripts/spotify.js get-playlist <playlist-url> --output round1.txt
node scripts/bingobaker.js create "Round 1 - Pop Divas" round1.txt

Bernie Sits

I made a website where people could put a picture of Bernie Sanders, sitting with his mittens on inauguration day, anywhere in the world with Google Maps street view. It went viral, hitting 9.8 million site requests in 4 days, and garnered some press attention as well. I immediately had 2 problems: scaling to meet the demand, and paying for the API requests to Google Maps. Luckily, I got in contact with some great engineers who taught me how to scale in real-time, and more than 7,000 people donated money to help pay for the costs. I learned a lot in this project, and hopefully gave some people a good meme along the way.

Here's a video I made sharing the story!

Fare Evasion

For my undergraduate thesis, I researched changes in fare evasion arrests in New York City's subway system as a result of new enforcement policies from the Manhattan District Attorney. For accessibility, I made an interactive map you can use to explore the data set I created for the paper. Feel free to click around! Read the paper here.

NYC Air Pollution

I noticed that I would cough a lot more during my commute when pollutant levels were high, so I did a brief survey of air quality in the city and its health impacts. This was one of my first projects about the city, and introduced me to the complexities of dealing with different types of map projections and geographic data. It's also a great example of how easy it is to turn around a project from NYC Open Data. Check out the jupyter notebook.
